### Hook patterns that are winning right now

1. **Curiosity + Community** — "The 'Weird Belt' Every Woman In My Fertility Group Is Wearing" — gold standard. Specific group + curiosity gap + social proof in one line.
2. **Anti-Alternative** — "What Injections Cannot Reach" / "Less Than a Single Clinic Visit" / "Here's why my sciatica surgery failed" — position against expensive/invasive alternatives.
3. **Problem Redefinition** — "The more I moisturized, the longer I ignored the real cause" (Skincare Today) — reframe what the audience thinks the problem is.
4. **Condition-Specific** — "Read This If You Have Neck Pain" / "NEW Carpal Tunnel Relief" — narrow targeting, high intent.
5. **Upgrade/Pro** — "Your Light Therapy Routine Just Went Pro" — speak to people already aware of red light therapy.
6. **Viral/FOMO** — "🚨 This Red Light Mat Is Going Viral" — urgency and social proof.
7. **Bold Guarantee** — "Firm Body or Money Back!" — risk reversal.
8. **Dual Authority** — "Clinically Backed, Mom Approved" — technical credibility + relatable validation.

### What NOBODY is doing (our gap)

- **Couples/together angle** — zero ads in the library mention partners, couples, or doing it together. Completely unoccupied territory.
- **British-specific positioning** — everything is US-focused. No one speaks to the British customer (British homes, British humour, British understatement).
- **Lifestyle/scenario selling** — almost everything is product-focused or problem-focused. Nobody sells the moment or the ritual.
- **Male-inclusive pain relief** — most belt ads target women. The couples angle naturally includes men without alienating the female audience.

## Angle 1: Couples ("Together") ⭐ PRIORITY

**Core idea:** Sell the scenario, not the product. Two people doing light therapy together on the sofa. Inspired by a real review — someone bought a second Belt for their husband so they can use them at the same time. Nobody in the space is doing this.

**Best product:** Relief Duo (£104.99 = two Relief Belts, 2nd belt half price).

**Offer mechanic:** £69.99 + £35.00 = £104.99 for two. Landed cost = 2× £23.80 = £47.60. Margin = 2.2× on the pair. AOV jumps from £70 to £105.

**Hook options (test 3-4):**
- "We bought two."
- "He stole mine, so I got him his own."
- "The 'weird belt' we both wear every evening."
- "Couples who recover together stay together."

**Why this should win:** Unique angle (zero competition), high AOV, emotional hook, review-backed, gender-neutral, and the creative itself (couple on sofa) is a pattern interrupt in a feed full of solo product shots.

## Angle 2: Anti-Alternative ("Skip the appointment")

**Core idea:** Position against expensive/invasive alternatives. This is a CONFIRMED winning pattern — multiple brands scaling with anti-clinic/anti-injection hooks.

**Steal from winners:**
- "What Injections Cannot Reach" (Gary Whitmore — active, scaling)
- "Less Than a Single Clinic Visit" (Well Past Forty — active)
- "Here's why my sciatica surgery failed" (Life After Back Pain — running weeks)

**Our version (Belt):**
- "Your physio charges £60 a session. This was £69.99."
- "No referral. No waiting list. No pills."
- "20 minutes. No appointment needed."

**Our version (Wand):**
- "My facialist charges £120. This was £49.99."
- "Salon-grade. Sofa-friendly."
- "Skip the clinic. Keep the results."

## Angle 3: Curiosity + Community (adapted from "Weird Belt")

**Core idea:** The "Weird Belt" hook is the single strongest pattern in the ad library. It works because it creates a curiosity gap AND social proof simultaneously. We adapt this for our audience.

**Our version:**
- "The 'weird red light thing' everyone in my gym is talking about."
- "My WhatsApp group won't shut up about this belt."
- "The thing every mum in my NCT group bought after I showed them mine."

**Why it works:** Creates an information gap (what is it?), implies community validation (everyone), and feels native/organic rather than salesy.

## Angle 4: Problem Redefinition

**Core idea:** Reframe what the audience thinks the solution is. "The more I moisturised, the longer I ignored the real cause" is actively running and scaling. This is powerful because it makes the viewer question their current approach.

**Our version (Wand):**
- "You don't need another serum. You need light."
- "The more I moisturised, the worse it got."
- "Every cream treats the surface. This goes deeper."

**Our version (Belt):**
- "Painkillers don't fix pain. They hide it."
- "Still stretching? The problem isn't flexibility."
- "Ibuprofen 3x a day wasn't a solution. It was a habit."

---

## Angle 5: Condition-Specific Targeting

**Core idea:** Narrow targeting, high intent. "Read This If You Have Neck Pain" is scaling with 4+ variants. Lead with the specific condition the audience already knows they have.

**Our version:**
- "Read this if your lower back hurts by 3pm."
- "For the knee that clicks every time you stand up."
- "This is for the morning stiffness nobody talks about."
- "Fine lines weren't there last year."

## Angle 6: The Quiet Ritual (Lifestyle/Brand)

**Core idea:** Sell the five minutes of calm, not the device. Brand-building, not hard-sell. Run low-budget alongside performance ads. This is our differentiator — nobody in the space does this.

**Hook:** "Five minutes. Every evening." (text overlay, no talking, ambient)

## Split-test plan (first wave)

Test 3 static concepts against each other in one ad set. Each concept gets 2 hook variants = 6 ads total. Let Meta's CBO pick the winner over 5 days.

| Concept | Hook A | Hook B |

Kill rules: £15 spend, zero ATCs → pause that ad. £30 spend, zero purchases → pause the ad set. Winner after 5 days gets scaled.
